## Why this amendment exists

The previous carrier-first program successfully established deterministic static and dynamic courts. Those courts can prove that a carrier lies inside the explicitly tested admissible set. They do not, by themselves, provide the corpus prior describing how production/artist animation meshes are usually discretized.

Therefore these are distinct claims:

- `Compiler PASS`: the candidate satisfies the frozen tested constraints/envelope;
- `TESSA prior`: the candidate is proposed from a distribution learned from coherent production meshes;
- `product mechanical truth`: only a Compiler-qualified TESSA/deterministic candidate that passes required downstream proofs.

A historical dynamic PASS must not be reinterpreted as proof that the carrier distribution is production-optimal or that the probe envelope was complete.

## New invariant

> Compiler admissibility is necessary but not a learned production-mesh prior; TESSA proposal quality is useful but never sufficient without Compiler proof.

The target system deliberately composes both:

`learned production-mesh prior + deterministic qualification/proof`.

## Stage18 semantic update

Stage18 no longer means only deterministic `GSA -> compact/replay/CDT carrier` construction.

Stage18-equivalent carrier construction may have two explicit proposal arms:

1. `DETERMINISTIC_BASELINE`: current provenance-preserving compact-face/CDT path;
2. `TESSA_PROPOSAL`: learned GSA-native production mesh proposal followed by mandatory Compiler support binding and qualification.

Both arms produce immutable carrier attempts. Neither arm may bypass Stage19-equivalent static qualification or downstream motion proof.

The deterministic arm remains mandatory as:
- scientific control;
- fallback;
- bounded repair primitive;
- regression baseline.

## Training-order consequence

Final ATLAS/MIRA retraining is blocked until the TESSA carrier evidence contract is frozen enough that downstream fits will not be invalidated by another carrier representation change.

Rig policy is independently fixed:
- source/raw skeleton truth is preserved where known;
- zero skin mass is not a deletion rule;
- historical sparse/pruned control courts remain optional optimization evidence, not source-rig authority.

## False-PASS response

TESSA does not excuse weaknesses in historical proof envelopes. The mechanical court must continue to expand toward continuous/material consequences, including where applicable:
- interior surface samples rather than vertices alone;
- deformation Jacobian residuals;
- orientation/fold evidence;
- stretch/shear/condition distributions;
- held-out articulation/stress poses;
- source/oracle consequence comparisons for reference assets.

The research question is not `TESSA versus Compiler`. It is whether the combination closes the observed gap:

`good static/dynamic gate metrics but visibly/mechanically poor animation`.
